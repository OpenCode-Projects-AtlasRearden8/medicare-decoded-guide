# 9. Glossary — Plain Words for Medicare Paper

Every definition here is **vocabulary, not instruction** — knowing what a
word means is different from advice, which this guide never gives. The
official definitions live in each plan's Evidence of Coverage; these are
the friendly translations.

*Education and paperwork help only — never medical, legal, financial, or
plan advice.*

| Term | Plain meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Accepts assignment | A provider's agreement to take Medicare's approved amount as full payment; the patient then owes at most the standard share |
| Advantage plan (Part C) | A private plan that bundles Part A and B (usually with D) into one plan with one card |
| ANOC (Annual Notice of Change) | The yearly fall letter that lists exactly what a plan is changing next year — costs, covered drugs, networks |
| Balance billing | A provider billing you for amounts beyond what insurance allows; largely blocked for QMB members and for providers that accept assignment |
| Benefit period | The Part A window that starts the day you're admitted and ends 60 days after discharge; a new one starts a new deductible |
| CMS | Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services — the federal agency that runs Medicare and writes its rules |
| Coinsurance | Your percentage share of a bill (imagine 20%) rather than a flat fee |
| Copay | A flat ticket price for a visit or item (imagine $20) |
| Creditable coverage | Drug coverage at least as good as Part D; having it means you can skip Part D without a late penalty |
| Custodial care | Long-term everyday help (bathing, dressing, supervision) — not a Medicare benefit under either door |
| Deductible | What you pay each year (or each benefit period) before the plan starts paying |
| Dual eligible | A person qualified for both Medicare and Medicaid; Medicare pays first, Medicaid fills much of the rest |
| EOC (Evidence of Coverage) | The plan's full legal contract — the phone-book document you consult like a dictionary |
| Extra Help | The federal (Low-Income Subsidy) program that slashes Part D drug costs for people with limited income; applied for through Social Security |
| Formulary | A plan's official drug list, organized into tiers |
| General Enrollment Period (GEP) | January 1 – March 31: the yearly second chance to sign up for A and B if you missed your initial window (coverage starts July 1) |
| In-network / out-of-network | Providers a private plan has a contract with (cheaper for you) versus everyone else (costlier, or not covered) |
| Initial Enrollment Period (IEP) | The 7 months around your 65th birthday (3 before, the birthday month, 3 after) when sign-ups are unhurried and penalty-free |
| IRMAA | Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount — the premium surcharge for higher incomes; the appeals process exists for life changes like retirement |
| Medicaid | The joint federal-state program for people with limited income; when paired with Medicare it fills much of what remains |
| Medigap | Private, standardized letter-plan supplements that fill the cost gaps in Original Medicare only (never with Advantage) |
| MSP (Medicare Savings Program) | State programs that pay some or all Medicare premiums (and at the strongest tier, deductibles and coinsurance) |
| Observation status | Being in a hospital bed without formal admission; bills under Part B, which changes your costs — you can ask which one you are |
| Open Enrollment (Fall) | October 15 – December 7 every year: the season for switching plans or doors, effective January 1 |
| Original Medicare | Parts A and B run directly by the government, leaving room to add Medigap and a Part D plan |
| Out-of-pocket maximum | The yearly ceiling on your cost-sharing in a private plan; after it, the plan pays 100% for covered in-network care |
| Part A | The hospital bucket: inpatient stays, short skilled-nursing stays, hospice, some home health |
| Part B | The doctors bucket: outpatient visits, labs, equipment, therapy, preventive services |
| Part C | The delivery of A and B (plus usually D) by a private Advantage plan |
| Part D | The prescription-drug bucket, sold by private plans with formularies and tiers |
| PACE | Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly — a wraparound program in some states for people who need nursing-level care but can live at home |
| Prior authorization | Plan approval required *before* a service or drug, or it may not be paid |
| Provider directory | The plan's list of doctors, hospitals, and pharmacies it works with |
| QI / QMB / SLMB | The three main Medicare Savings Program tiers: QI and SLMB pay the Part B premium; QMB also pays A premiums, deductibles, and coinsurance, with strong billing protections |
| SEP (Special Enrollment Period) | An off-cycle window triggered by life events — losing other coverage, moving, qualifying for Extra Help, and others |
| SHIP | State Health Insurance Assistance Program — free, unbiased, trained counselors who sell nothing and explain everything |
| Skilled nursing facility (SNF) | Short-term rehab care after a hospital stay that Part A can cover; different from a nursing home for long-term custodial care |
| Social Security (SSA) | The federal office that enrolls you in Parts A and B, handles Extra Help applications, and administers benefit payments |
| Star ratings | Medicare's 1-to-5 yearly service-quality scores for plans — a data point, not a personal recommendation |
| Step therapy | A plan rule requiring you to try (and document failure of) cheaper drugs before a costlier one is covered |
| Tiers (drug) | Formulary cost groups: tier 1 cheapest generics, then preferred brands, then specialty tiers |
| Underwriting | An insurer's health-based decision to accept, price, or decline you — normal for Medigap outside open enrollment, forbidden during it |
| Worst-case planning | Comparing plans by their yearly ceiling (out-of-pocket maximum or supplement behavior) rather than by premiums alone |

signal to keep [Start Here](index.md) bookmarked — the guide's purpose is
to keep you from needing this table very often.
