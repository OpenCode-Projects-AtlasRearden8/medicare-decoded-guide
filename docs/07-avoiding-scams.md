# 7. Avoiding Plan-Switching Scams

## Why Medicare has its own scam chapter

Medicare enrollment season is scam season. The stakes (health coverage),
the audience (tens of millions of people with a birthday deadline), and
the paperwork complexity make a fertile swamp. This chapter covers the
two kinds of harm:

1. **Sneaky sign-ups and switches** — being enrolled in a plan you never
   chose, or switched out of one you did choose.
2. **Classic fraud** — extracting your Medicare number, money, or
   identity with fear and urgency.

Both are survivable if you keep two reflexes: **slow down** and **call
the official number yourself**.

*Education and paperwork help only — never medical, legal, financial, or
plan advice.*

## The three golden rules

1. **Medicare does not call you unless you asked.** Out of the blue, no
   federal Medicare employee will phone to "review your benefits" or
   "deliver your new card."
2. **Medicare does not sell plans.** Anyone saying "I'm calling from
   Medicare about your plan options" is not calling from Medicare. Plans
   are sold by private companies (and explained for free by SHIP).
3. **Never read your Medicare number aloud to someone who called you.**
   Treat the Medicare number exactly like a credit card. If *you*
   initiated the call to a verified number, giving what's needed to
   verify identity is normal. On an incoming call, it never is.

## The scam shapes, named

| Scam shape | What it sounds like | The tell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The gift-card offer | "You qualify for a $500 grocery/gas card — just confirm your Medicare number" | Real programs never pay in gift cards, and benefits never require reading card digits to a stranger |
| The fear switch | "Your plan is being canceled today unless you act now" | No real deadline works that way; Fall Open Enrollment and SEPs [come back](02-enrollment-windows.md) |
| The fake deadline | "This is a one-time window" | The real windows are public and repeating; urgency on the phone is the product |
| The too-good plan | "This plan includes free groceries, dental, and $0 everything — let me switch you right now" | Perks exist, but only the Summary of Benefits is binding — [read the fine print](05-reading-fine-print.md) before any switch |
| The "we're from Medicare" plan rep | Uses Medicare's name to sell a private plan | Medicare does not sell; hang up and call 1-800-MEDICARE yourself |
| The quiet paper trap | A "free lunch" or "health survey" sign-up sheet whose fine print is a **permission slip** allowing sales calls and, in some cases, plan changes | Permission slips are legal; revoke in writing and stop answering that number |

## Illegal switching: what it looks like

Being switched without consent is a real, reportable harm. You would
notice it through paper, not vibes:

- A **new insurance card** arrives for a plan you never contacted.
- Fall mail includes an **ANOC from a plan you don't recognize**
  ([what an ANOC is](05-reading-fine-print.md)).
- On January 1, your **drug plan or doctor network changed** with no
  action from you.
- A pharmacy suddenly "can't find" your coverage.
- A family member reports a change "was taken care of for you."

## What to do if it already happened

A calm first-aid list, in order:

1. **Ask the question that fixes everything:** call **1-800-MEDICARE**
   and say, plainly: *"Please tell me exactly what plan I am currently
   enrolled in, when it took effect, and who authorized it."* Write the
   answers in your call log.
2. **If the answer is wrong**, say: *"I did not authorize this change.
   I want it corrected and my previous coverage restored."* There are
   special windows and processes for non-consented changes — the phone
   representative can route yours, and a SHIP counselor can help you
   push if the first attempt stalls.
3. **Document everything** — dates, names, confirmation numbers — in the
   [call-notes worksheet](worksheets/plan-call-notes-worksheet.md).
4. **Report it:** 1-800-MEDICARE can log the complaint; your **SHIP**
   office and your **state insurance department** can escalate. Report
   even if it was fixed — patterns matter.
5. **Check the rest of the paperwork** for other unauthorized changes
   (address, bank deposit routing) — a switch often travels with other
   identity touches.

## Scam-proofing checklist

Print this, or copy it into your
[binder notes](08-enrollment-binder.md):

```
[ ] I never give my Medicare number to incoming callers.
[ ] I hang up and dial the official number myself:
      1-800-MEDICARE, Social Security, or my plan's member line.
[ ] I never pay a fee to "apply for Medicare benefits."
[ ] I read any sign-up sheet before signing - permission slips
      allow sales calls.
[ ] I compare from plan papers, not ads.
[ ] I keep my card at home except at appointments.
[ ] I log every Medicare-related call with date + name + number.
[ ] If something feels off, I say: "I need to think about it.
      Mail me the details." and end the call.
```

## The mindset that beats the whole industry

Every legitimate Medicare fact survives a two-week delay. Every fact
that dies under a two-week delay was never a fact — it was a pressure
tactic. "Mail me the details" is a complete sentence, and your binder
keeps the paper trail that makes it stick.
