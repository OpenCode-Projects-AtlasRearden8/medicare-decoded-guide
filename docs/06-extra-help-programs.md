# 6. Extra Help and Other Lifelines

## The quiet programs almost nobody applies for

If the costs in the last few chapters made your stomach sink, this is the
chapter that matters most. There are **free, official programs** that
reduce Medicare costs dramatically for people with modest incomes — and
by most accounts, a large share of people who qualify never apply. This
chapter explains what exists, in plain words, and where to apply.

**Everything here is education.** Eligibility rules have income and
resource limits that change by year and state; the official offices
listed at the end make the actual decisions.

*Education and paperwork help only — never medical, legal, financial, or
plan advice.*

## Program 1: "Extra Help" (also called the Low-Income Subsidy)

**What it is:** a federal program that pays a large share of Part D drug
costs — premiums, deductibles, and copays — for people with limited
income and resources.

**In round teaching numbers:** imagine a drug bill of $2,400 a year
(premiums plus fills). With Extra Help approved, the same year's drug
costs might drop to something like $100 a year. That is the shape of the
help — dramatic, not incremental.

**What makes it worth knowing:**

- You can **apply any day of the year** through **Social Security**
  (online, by phone, or in an office).
- Approval comes with **year-round special enrollment rights** in Part D
  plans — one of the few truly panic-free windows in all of Medicare
  (see [Enrollment Windows, Calmly](02-enrollment-windows.md)).
- Social Security automatically forwards your information to your state
  for the next program, below.

## Program 2: Medicare Savings Programs (MSPs)

**What they are:** state-run programs that pay some or all of your
Medicare premiums — and in the strongest tier, your deductibles and
coinsurance too.

| MSP tier | What it pays (plainly) |
| --- | --- |
| **QMB** (Qualified Medicare Beneficiary) | Your Part A and B premiums, plus your deductibles, coinsurance, and copays — and as a legal bonus, providers generally cannot bill you beyond Medicare's limits |
| **SLMB** (Specified Low-Income Medicare Beneficiary) | Your Part B premium |
| **QI** (Qualifying Individual) | Your Part B premium, funded year by year with limited slots — apply early |

**Where to apply:** your **state Medicaid office** (often the same
office that handles other assistance). Rules on income and resources
vary by state, and some states are far more generous than the federal
floor — which is exactly why "check your own state" beats "I heard I
wouldn't qualify."

## Program 3: Medicaid, when both programs apply

Some people qualify for **both Medicare and Medicaid** (often called
being *dual eligible*). In plain terms: **Medicare pays first; Medicaid
fills much of what remains** — sometimes premiums, sometimes long-term
services that Medicare never covered, depending on the state and the
person. If your income is low, this is worth an actual application,
because dual status usually also unlocks extra help choosing and
switching plans year-round.

## Program 4: PACE, in one paragraph

PACE (Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly) is a special
program in some states for people 55+ who need nursing-home-level care
but can live safely at home, wrapping medical care, home care, and
transportation into one plan. It is a big topic with strict eligibility,
so here it is only flagged as existing — a SHIP counselor or your state
Medicaid office can say whether PACE operates near you.

## Where to apply, all in one place

| Program | Where you apply |
| --- | --- |
| Extra Help (drug costs) | **Social Security** — online at ssa.gov, by phone, or in an office |
| Medicare Savings Programs | **Your state Medicaid office** |
| Medicaid (dual coverage) | **Your state Medicaid office** |
| Free, unbiased guidance on all of the above | **SHIP** — your State Health Insurance Assistance Program |
| "What am I actually enrolled in?" | **1-800-MEDICARE** |

## A worked round-number picture

All numbers invented, for shape only:

| Yearly drug + premium bill (teaching) | Without help | With Extra Help approved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drug plan premium | $420 | near $0 |
| Deductible | $300 | $0 |
| 12 fills | $1,680 | small flat copays, roughly $100 total |
| **Year total** | **$2,400** | **about $100–$200** |

And if a **QMB-level MSP** also applies, add the Part B premium savings
(imagine $2,000 a year) and protection from most Medicare-related
bills beyond official limits. For a person with modest income, these
programs together can be worth thousands of dollars a year — which is
why the extra half hour to apply is the highest-paying paperwork in this
entire guide.

## Three rules that keep you safe while applying

1. **Applying is always free.** Anyone who charges a fee to "find your
   Medicare benefits" or "check your eligibility" is waving a red flag
   — see [Avoiding Plan-Switching Scams](07-avoiding-scams.md).
2. **The official offices never call first to ask for your Medicare
   number or bank details.** You contact them; they do not cold-call
   you.
3. **Keep copies of every application and approval letter** in your
   [enrollment binder](08-enrollment-binder.md). Approvals drive
   year-round enrollment rights; losing the letter means proving it all
   again.

*Education and paperwork help only — never medical, legal, financial, or
