# 8. Your Own Enrollment Binder

## The pile becomes tabs

Over one year, Medicare paperwork will mail you roughly twenty items:
the ANOC, plan summaries, drug-plan letters, penalty notices (if any),
confirmation letters, explanations of benefits, tax-year statements.
Loose, this pile is a nightmare. **Bindered, it is a filing system you
fully control** — and the single most calming Medicare purchase there is.

This chapter builds one binder in one sitting, with six tabs.

*Education and paperwork help only — never medical, legal, financial, or
plan advice.*

## The shopping list

- One **1.5–2 inch three-ring binder** (or an accordion folder — the
  tabs concept is identical)
- **6 tab dividers** (the write-on kind)
- A pack of **sheet protectors** for cards and precious letters
- A **pencil pouch** with pens and a pad of sticky notes
- Optional: a **scanner app or flatbed scanner** for backup copies

Total cost: about $15. Total setup time: one hour, once a year to
maintain.

## The six tabs

| Tab | Label | What goes inside |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Contacts** | Plan member-services numbers (from your cards), 1-800-MEDICARE, your SHIP office, your Social Security office, your pharmacy. One page. |
| 2 | **Current plan** | Your current plan card (or a color copy of it), Summary of Benefits, this year's ANOC, the formulary pages for your drugs, provider-directory pages for your doctors |
| 3 | **Enrollment records** | Confirmation letters, effective dates, penalty notices, Extra Help / MSP approval letters — anything proving *what you are in and when it started* |
| 4 | **Letters in and out** | Every Medicare-related letter that arrives, and copies of anything you mail or dispute. Oldest at the back. |
| 5 | **Money papers** | Premium statements, payment confirmations, yearly tax statements (the Social Security one), drug receipts for the tax year |
| 6 | **Notes & worksheets** | Your filled [deadlines worksheet](worksheets/enrollment-deadlines-worksheet.md), [decision worksheet](worksheets/decision-worksheet-advantage-or-supplement.md), [call notes](worksheets/plan-call-notes-worksheet.md), and [binder inventory](worksheets/binder-inventory-worksheet.md) |

That's the whole architecture. Six tabs, everything lands somewhere.

## The yearly ritual (one hour, twice)

**Every fall (October–December),** during Open Enrollment:

1. Pull Tab 2. Read the new ANOC with
   [Reading a Plan's Fine Print](05-reading-fine-print.md) — three pages:
   the changes summary, the cost table, your top drugs' formulary rows.
2. Decide nothing under pressure; fill the
   [decision worksheet](worksheets/decision-worksheet-advantage-or-supplement.md)
   if a change matters.
3. Call SHIP if the math is complicated. They are free and sell nothing.
4. Archive last year's papers to the back of Tab 4, and start Tab 2 fresh.

**Every January:**

1. Replace Tab 2's card copy with the new year's card.
2. File the confirmation letters of any changes in Tab 3.
3. Mark your new [deadlines worksheet](worksheets/enrollment-deadlines-worksheet.md).

## Privacy and shredding rules

The binder holds identity-grade paper. Four rules keep it safe:

- **The Medicare number is credit-card-grade.** Never leave the binder
  in the car; never email scans of the card; store the binder where
  you'd store a wallet.
- **Keep the card *copy* in the binder, the card itself in your wallet**
  (carried only to medical appointments — it is not an everyday ID).
- **Shred, don't trash,** old cards and expired plan papers — after the
  tax year passes and you have the replacements confirmed in hand.
- **Keep one thing out of the binder:** full banking details. If a form
  requires them, complete and submit it; keep only the submitted-date
  record, not the account numbers, in your files.

## The digital backup, in five minutes

- Photograph (or scan) the card, the current Summary of Benefits, and
  the year's ANOC confirmation.
- Name files plainly: `medicare-card-2026.jpg`,
  `plan-summary-2026.pdf`.
- Store them **offline** — a personal folder on your own computer or a
  personal external drive. No email attachments, no public cloud
  folders, no sharing links.
- If you use a password manager or encrypted drive, better still; if
  not, an offline folder on a machine you control is already fine for
  this purpose.

## Low-energy mode

Built for the tiredest version of you:

- **Fifteen minutes a month.** Once a month, open the binder, drop new
  papers into the right tabs, done. No organizing beyond filing.
- **One tab at a time.** If the pile is huge, do Tab 2 today, Tab 4 next
  week. Tabs are independent.
- **"Done" means the folder exists.** A messy binder beats a perfect
  pile. You can always re-sort later during a TV episode.
- **The [binder inventory worksheet](worksheets/binder-inventory-worksheet.md)**
  is a checklist you can hand to a helper, so someone else can assemble
  it with you or for you.

## Why this chapter is the payoff

Every other chapter hands you paper — the ANOC, the confirmations, the
approvals. The binder is where those chapters land. When a scam caller
pushes, when a hospital asks what plan you have, when a SHIP counselor
needs your drug list, when April-you wonders what November-you decided —
the answer is a six-tab binder, and it takes one hour to own.
