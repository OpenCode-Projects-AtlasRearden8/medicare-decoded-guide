# 1. Medicare in One Picture

## What Medicare is, in one paragraph

Medicare is **federal health insurance** — a health program run by the
United States government. It is mostly for people 65 and older, and also for
some younger people with certain conditions. It is not welfare and it is not
charity; you (or a spouse) generally pay into it for years through work, and
then it is yours. It has **parts** with single-letter names, and once you
understand what each letter buys, the whole maze gets quiet.

*Education and paperwork help only — never medical, legal, financial, or
plan advice.*

## The one picture

```
                  WHAT MEDICARE IS, IN ONE PICTURE

              MEDICARE (federal health insurance)
                              |
           ---------------------------------------------
           |                                           |
     ORIGINAL MEDICARE                          MEDICARE ADVANTAGE
     (the government runs it)                  (a private plan runs
           |                                    everything, Part C)
           |                                           |
   +-------+--------+                      one plan, one card,
   |       |        |                      usually A + B + D
   A       B        D                      bundled together
 hospital  doctors  drugs
   |
   + you may ADD, privately:
       Medigap (fills Original's gaps)  +  Part D plan (drugs)
```

Read the picture left to right and it says this: there are **two doors**.

- **Door 1 — Original Medicare.** The government runs Parts A and B for you.
  You can add a **Medigap** supplement (a private policy that fills the
  cost gaps) and a **Part D** drug plan. You carry three cards' worth of
  arrangements and can use almost any doctor who takes Medicare.
- **Door 2 — Medicare Advantage (Part C).** A private company takes your
  A, B, and usually D and runs them together as one plan with one card.
  It may add extras. In exchange, you typically use its network and follow
  its rules.

Every year — forever — you get to stand at those two doors again during
[the fall enrollment window](02-enrollment-windows.md). Nothing is
forever-except-the-fall-window.

## The four letters, in one breath

- **Part A** — the *hospital* part: inpatient care, short skilled-nursing
  stays, hospice, some home health. Most people have paid for it already
  through work and owe no monthly premium.
- **Part B** — the *doctors* part: office visits, outpatient care, lab
  work, medical equipment, preventive visits. It has a monthly premium
  that can rise with income.
- **Part C** — not a benefit but a *delivery*: a private Medicare
  Advantage plan that bundles A and B (and usually D) into one plan.
- **Part D** — the *drugs* part, sold by private insurers with yearly
  drug lists and tiers.

See [Parts A–D, Translated](03-parts-a-through-d.md) for each in plain
words with round numbers.

## What Original Medicare typically does not cover

This trips up almost everyone, so it belongs in the picture:

- **Long-term care** — everyday custodial help (bathing, dressing,
  around-the-clock supervision) is *not* a Medicare benefit. This is the
  single biggest surprise. Short rehab stays after a hospital stay can be
  covered; ongoing custodial care is a separate, big topic.
- **Most dental, hearing aids, and routine eye care** (eyeglasses to see
  with are usually not covered; glasses after eye surgery sometimes are).
- **Care outside the United States** — almost always on you.

Advantage plans often add some dental/vision/hearing perks — that is one
of their selling points — but "typically" is the operative word for both
doors. **Your own plan papers are the final word, always.** If a brochure
and a government summary disagree, ask 1-800-MEDICARE.

## The cast of characters

| Who | What they actually do |
| --- | --- |
| **Medicare (CMS)** | The federal agency that writes the rules, sets the standards, and runs Original Medicare. It does not sell plans. |
| **Social Security (SSA)** | The sign-up office. You enroll in A and B through Social Security, online or in person. |
| **Private plans** | Sell Advantage plans, Part D drug plans, and Medigap policies. Salespeople work *for the plans*, not for you. |
| **SHIP** | State Health Insurance Assistance Program. Free, unbiased, trained human counselors who can explain your options without selling anything. Underused on purpose, as far as we can tell. |
| **1-800-MEDICARE** | The official helpline. Can tell you what plan you are actually in and what changed. |
| **Your providers** | Hospitals, clinics, pharmacies. They bill, and they can tell you whether they take a given plan. |
| **Your binder** | The hero of this guide. See [Your Own Enrollment Binder](08-enrollment-binder.md). |

## Round-number vocabulary (the only five words you need)

| Word | Plain meaning | Teaching number |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Premium | The monthly "rent" you pay to have the insurance | $100 a month |
| Deductible | What you pay each year before the plan starts helping | $500 at the start of the year |
| Copay | A flat ticket price per visit or item | $20 per visit |
| Coinsurance | Your percentage share of the bill | 20% of what's left |
| Out-of-pocket maximum | The yearly ceiling after which the plan pays 100% | $5,000 worst case |

With those five words you can read nearly every Medicare document in
existence. The [Glossary](09-glossary.md) adds the rest when you want it.
